# Phishing Recognition Cheat Sheet

## Quick Reference Card

### Red Flags in Emails

| Warning Sign | Example | What To Do |
|-------------|---------|-------------|
| **Urgent Action Required** | "Your account will be terminated today unless..." | Verify through official channels, not via the email |
| **Grammar/Spelling Errors** | "Dear valued costumer, your acount needs..." | Be suspicious of unprofessional communications |
| **Mismatched URLs** | Text shows "paypal.com" but link is "paypa1.com" | Hover over links to see actual destination before clicking |
| **Request for Sensitive Info** | "Please confirm your password and credit card..." | Legitimate companies never ask for passwords via email |
| **Unexpected Attachments** | Random invoice or shipping document | Don't open attachments you weren't expecting |
| **Generic Greeting** | "Dear User" instead of your name | Be wary of impersonal greetings from services that know you |
| **Email from Executive** | CEO asking for urgent wire transfer | Verify unusual requests through another channel |

### What To Check

1. **Sender Email Address**: Look carefully for subtle misspellings (microsoft-support.com vs microsoft.com)
2. **Hover Before Clicking**: Always hover over links to see the actual URL
3. **Check for HTTPS**: Secure sites should have a padlock icon in the browser
4. **Unexpected Communications**: Be extra cautious with emails you weren't expecting
5. **Too Good To Be True**: Unexpected winnings, inheritances, or deals are usually scams

## Common Phishing Scenarios

### Finance-Related Phishing
- Fake invoices requiring immediate payment
- Tax refund notifications
- Banking alerts about "suspicious activity"

### Account-Related Phishing
- "Your account has been limited"
- Password reset emails you didn't request
- Account verification required

### Delivery/Shipping Scams
- Package delivery notifications when you're not expecting anything
- Customs fees for international packages
- Failed delivery attempt notices

### CEO/Executive Fraud
- Urgent requests from executives for gift cards or wire transfers
- Requests that bypass normal procedures
- Messages emphasizing secrecy or confidentiality

## Example of a Phishing Email vs. Legitimate Email

### Phishing Email
- Generic greeting
- Creates urgency
- Contains subtle errors
- Asks for sensitive information
- Has suspicious links or attachments
- Sender address doesn't match company domain

### Legitimate Email
- Personalized greeting
- Clear business purpose
- Professional writing
- Directs to official website (not via embedded links)
- Contact information matches known details
- No urgent threats or promises

## Remember the S.T.O.P Method

- **S**top and think before clicking or responding
- **T**hink about whether this makes sense or seems suspicious
- **O**bserve sender details, links and attachments carefully
- **P**rotect your information - when in doubt, don't share
