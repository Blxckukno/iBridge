# Phishing Prevention Guide

## What is Phishing?

Phishing is a cybercrime where attackers disguise themselves as trustworthy entities to trick victims into revealing sensitive information such as:
- Usernames and passwords
- Credit card details
- Banking information
- Corporate access credentials

## Recent Incident

Our company recently experienced a phishing attack where:
- The CEO's email account was compromised
- External parties sent phishing emails from the legitimate account
- An "impossible travel" alert was triggered for another user account

## How to Identify Phishing Emails

### Red Flags to Watch For:

1. **Urgency and Pressure Tactics**
   - "Immediate action required"
   - "Account will be closed unless you act now"
   - Threats of negative consequences

2. **Suspicious Sender Information**
   - Email addresses that mimic but don't exactly match legitimate domains
   - Names that don't match email addresses
   - Unfamiliar or unexpected senders claiming to be colleagues

3. **Poor Grammar and Spelling**
   - Professional organizations typically proofread their communications
   - Multiple spelling and grammar errors can indicate phishing

4. **Suspicious Links and Attachments**
   - Hover over links to see the actual URL before clicking
   - Be wary of shortened URLs (bit.ly, tinyurl.com, etc.)
   - Unexpected attachments, especially executable files (.exe, .zip, .bat)

5. **Requests for Sensitive Information**
   - Legitimate organizations rarely ask for sensitive information via email
   - Requests for password changes, financial details, or personal data

6. **Generic Greetings**
   - "Dear User," "Dear Customer," or "Dear Account Holder" instead of your name
   - Organizations you do business with typically know your name

7. **Mismatched URLs**
   - Links that claim to go to a legitimate site but point elsewhere
   - URLs with slight misspellings (e.g., microsoft-secure.com instead of microsoft.com)

8. **Unexpected Emails**
   - Emails about accounts you don't have
   - Unexpected package deliveries or purchases you didn't make
   - Unexpected password reset requests

2. Click the "Report Phishing" button in the toolbar
3. The email will be reported to our security team and safely removed from your inbox

## Best Practices for Email Security

1. **Verify Unusual Requests**
   - Confirm unusual requests (especially financial ones) through a different channel
   - Call the sender directly using a known phone number (not one provided in the email)
   - For internal emails, verify in person or via Teams/Slack when possible

2. **Use Multi-Factor Authentication (MFA)**
   - Enable MFA on all your accounts
   - Even if credentials are compromised, attackers can't access accounts without the second factor

3. **Keep Software Updated**
   - Ensure your operating system, browsers, and applications are up to date
   - Updates often include security patches for known vulnerabilities

4. **Use Strong, Unique Passwords**
   - Use different passwords for different accounts
   - Consider using a password manager like Bitwarden or KeePass

5. **Be Cautious with Personal Information**
   - Minimize the personal information you share online
   - Attackers can use personal details to craft convincing phishing emails

6. **Check Email Headers**
   - Learn how to examine email headers to verify the true source
   - Report suspicious headers to IT security

7. **Don't Trust Displays Names**
   - Display names can be easily spoofed
   - Always verify the actual email address

## What to Do If You Think You've Been Phished

1. **Don't Panic, But Act Quickly**
   - The faster you respond, the better chance of minimizing damage

2. **Report the Incident**
   - Notify IT Security immediately via email at security@ibridgesa.co.za
   - Call the IT Security Desk at extension 5555

3. **Change Your Passwords**
   - Change passwords for any accounts you suspect may be compromised
   - Start with your email account, then financial accounts, then others

4. **Monitor Your Accounts**
   - Watch for suspicious activity on your email, financial, and other accounts
   - Enable alerts for account activity when possible

5. **Scan Your Computer**
   - Run a full virus and malware scan on your computer
   - We've installed security tools that can help with this
